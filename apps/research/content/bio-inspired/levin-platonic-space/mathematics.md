# Mathematics

[Guide](/notes/bio-inspired/levin-platonic-space/) · [Talks](/notes/bio-inspired/levin-platonic-space/talks/) · [Papers](/notes/bio-inspired/levin-platonic-space/papers/) · [Books](/notes/bio-inspired/levin-platonic-space/books/) · Mathematics · [People and labs](/notes/bio-inspired/levin-platonic-space/people/)

This page lists the mathematics that Michael Levin uses in his argument about Platonic space and in his papers. Each item gives his use, the standard statement with a source, and a verdict on his statement. The verdicts are our judgement. Most of his written statements are correct, and the errors come mostly in unscripted talk.

## Learning path, in order {#learning-path}

1. **Proof, sets and number systems.** Learn to read and write proofs and how the numbers are built from sets. Resources: [Hammack, Book of Proof](https://richardhammack.github.io/BookOfProof/); [Tao, Analysis I](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-981-10-1789-6). Lets you judge: His claim that e follows from the empty set, his number facts (cannonball, 37, primes, perfect numbers) and the step that axioms force mathematical facts.
2. **Algebra and symmetry.** Learn when equations can be solved in radicals and what the division algebras are. Resources: [Stewart, Galois Theory](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL175884W); [Baez, The Octonions](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0105155); [Zee, Fearful Symmetry](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL485891W). Lets you judge: His claims about formulas in radicals, quaternions and octonions, symmetry groups and the amplituhedron.
3. **Logic and computability.** Learn how logic is built from NAND gates and what Turing machines can and cannot decide. Resources: [Nisan and Schocken, The Elements of Computing Systems](https://www.nand2tetris.org/book); [Sipser, Introduction to the Theory of Computation](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19859284W). Lets you judge: His claims about NAND logic, the halting problem, Gödel limits and the paper on sorting algorithms.
4. **Probability.** Learn the binomial distribution, the law of large numbers and the central limit theorem. Resources: [Blitzstein and Hwang, Introduction to Probability](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL22320351W). Lets you judge: His claims about the Galton board and runs in random sequences.
5. **Dynamical systems, chaos and fractals.** Learn fixed points, bifurcations, the Feigenbaum constant, fractals and strange attractors. Resources: [Strogatz, Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos](https://doi.org/10.1201/9780429492563); [Peitgen, Jürgens and Saupe, Chaos and Fractals](https://doi.org/10.1007/b97624). Lets you judge: His claims about the Feigenbaum constant, the Halley fractal, the liar paradox as a dynamical system, attractors, learning gene networks and the Lenia paper.
6. **Variational physics and the thermodynamics of computation.** Learn the principle of stationary action and the minimum energy cost of erasing a bit. Resources: [Lanczos, The Variational Principles of Mechanics](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2020255W); [Landauer 1961](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.53.0183). Lets you judge: His claim that least action is minimal goal-directedness and his claims about the cost of computation.
7. **Information theory and causal emergence.** Learn entropy and mutual information, then the measures of causal emergence. Resources: [Cover and Thomas, Elements of Information Theory](https://doi.org/10.1002/047174882X); [Williams and Beer 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.2515); [Hoel, Albantakis and Tononi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1314922110); [Mediano et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2423297122). Lets you judge: His claim that mathematics gives free gifts through causal emergence, and the paper on the causally emergent alignment hypothesis.
8. **Causation and counterfactuals.** Learn how counterfactuals work in causal models and why counterfactuals about mathematics are a special problem. Resources: [Pearl, Glymour and Jewell, Causal Inference in Statistics: A Primer](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20125998W); [Baron, Colyvan and Ripley 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/philmat/nkz023). Lets you judge: His claim that patterns are causes, as in the case of primes and cicadas.
9. **Free-energy principle and Markov blankets.** Learn active inference and the debate about what Markov blankets mean. Resources: [Parr, Pezzulo and Friston, Active Inference](https://doi.org/10.7551/mitpress/12441.001.0001); [Bruineberg et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X21002351). Lets you judge: The claims in his papers about the boundaries of agents.
10. **Generative models and learned representations.** Learn variational autoencoders, diffusion models and the Platonic representation hypothesis. Resources: [Kingma and Welling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114); [Ho et al., Denoising diffusion probabilistic models](https://arxiv.org/abs/2006.11239); [Huh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987). Lets you judge: His claim that the genome is a compressed code, the paper on diffusion models as evolutionary algorithms, and his use of the representation hypothesis.
11. **Category theory and sheaves.** Learn categories, universal properties and sheaves. Resources: [Fong and Spivak, Seven Sketches](https://arxiv.org/abs/1803.05316); [Riehl, Category Theory in Context](https://emilyriehl.github.io/files/context.pdf); [Abramsky and Brandenburger 2011](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/13/11/113036). Lets you judge: The claims about sheaves and contextuality, universal properties as governors, and his proposed algebra of perspectives.
12. **Minimal models.** Learn cellular automata, Lenia and neural cellular automata. Resources: [Chan 2019, Lenia](https://doi.org/10.25088/ComplexSystems.28.3.251); [Mordvintsev et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.23915/distill.00023); [Beer 2014, The cognitive domain of a glider](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00125). Lets you judge: His claims about gliders, his papers on sorting algorithms and Lenia, and his plan to study Platonic space with minimal models.

## His examples {#his-examples}

- **Triangle angle sum.** His statement: correct. He says that evolution gets the third angle of a triangle free, which saves a third of the work. Standard statement: In flat (Euclidean) geometry the angles of a triangle sum to 180 degrees, so two angles fix the third. This is false on curved surfaces. [Wikipedia: Sum of angles of a triangle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sum_of_angles_of_a_triangle). In the paper he adds the condition of flat space. The claim that this saves a third of the work is rhetoric.
- **Periodical cicadas and prime numbers.** His statement: loose. He says that the 13-year and 17-year cycles of cicadas are prime, so the chain of why-questions ends in mathematics. Standard statement: Magicicada broods come out every 13 or 17 years. Why the periods are prime is a hypothesis (predator avoidance or less hybrid mixing) that models support but do not establish. [Wikipedia: Periodical cicadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodical_cicadas). The numbers are right, but he states the prime explanation as fact when it is still a hypothesis.
- **Cicadas at 7 and 11 years.** His statement: wrong. In one interview he gives the cicada periods as seven and 11 years. Standard statement: The periods are 13 and 17 years. [Wikipedia: Periodical cicadas](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Periodical_cicadas). This is a slip of the tongue. He gives 13 and 17 in other places.
- **The value of e said as 2.78.** His statement: loose. He says that e equals 2.78 as an example of a number that no one chooses. Standard statement: e = 2.71828... [MathWorld: e](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/e.html). Three recordings give 2.78, so it is a spoken slip, and in one talk he corrects it to 2.718.
- **e called the value of the natural logarithm.** His statement: loose. He calls e the value of the natural logarithm. Standard statement: e is the base of the natural logarithm, so the natural logarithm of e is 1. [MathWorld: e](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/e.html). He uses the wrong noun for the right number.
- **e forced by the empty set and the successor.** His statement: loose. He says that from minimal assumptions you discover e, and that you cannot choose its value. Standard statement: The natural numbers can be built from zero and a successor operation. To reach e you also need the real numbers and limits, for example e as the limit of (1 + 1/n) to the power n. [Tao, Analysis I](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL8016096W). It is standard that axioms force the value of e, but the empty set and successor alone are not enough.
- **e and pi cannot be changed by physics.** His statement: correct. He says that a change in the physical constants at the Big Bang cannot change e. Standard statement: e and pi have mathematical definitions. No physical constant enters these definitions. [MathWorld: Pi](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/Pi.html). This is true of the definitions. To go from this fact to Platonism is a separate philosophical step.
- **Feigenbaum constant.** His statement: correct. He uses the Feigenbaum constant, about 4.6692, as a prime example of a free fact that no physics could change. Standard statement: The constant 4.669201... is the limit ratio of successive period-doubling intervals. It is the same for all maps with one smooth hump. [MathWorld: Feigenbaum Constant](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/FeigenbaumConstant.html). He states the value and its universality correctly.
- **Four-colour theorem.** His statement: correct. He says that physics cannot explain why a map needs four colours and not nine. Standard statement: Every planar map can be coloured with at most four colours so that regions that touch have different colours. [Wikipedia: Four color theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Four_color_theorem). When he says any map, he means a planar map, and for those the claim is right.
- **Khinchin's constant.** His statement: correct. He gives the number 2.685 as a specific value that has no physical explanation. Standard statement: For almost all real numbers, the geometric mean of the continued-fraction terms tends to 2.685452... [MathWorld: Khinchin's Constant](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/KhinchinsConstant.html). The value is right.
- **Cannonball problem.** His statement: correct. He says that a square number of balls can make a square pyramid only when the side is 70. Standard statement: The sum of the squares from 1 to 24 is 4900, which is 70 squared. This is the only case apart from the trivial case of one ball. [MathWorld: Cannonball Problem](https://mathworld.wolfram.com/CannonballProblem.html). The claim is right but leaves out the trivial case.
- **ABABAB is divisible by 37.** His statement: correct. He gives this as a bare fact of number theory. Standard statement: ABABAB equals AB times 10101, and 10101 equals 3 times 7 times 13 times 37. . The arithmetic is right.
- **Primes next to a multiple of 6.** His statement: correct. He says that every prime larger than 3 sits next to a multiple of 6. Standard statement: Every prime larger than 3 has the form 6k plus 1 or 6k minus 1. [Wikipedia: Prime number](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Prime_number). The claim is right.
- **Perfect numbers and a massive jump.** His statement: wrong. He says that the first six perfect numbers are close together and that a massive jump comes after the sixth. Standard statement: The values he gives are right. The sixth is about 256 times the fifth, but the seventh is only about 16 times the sixth. [OEIS A000396](https://oeis.org/A000396). The jump to the seventh perfect number is smaller in ratio than the jump before it.
- **Mathematical constants cluster between 0.5 and 5.** His statement: loose. He asks why mathematical constants cluster between about 0.5 and 5, and suggests that this may show a structure of Platonic space. Standard statement: The short table of selected constants that he linked lies between 0.57 and 4.67, apart from 0 and 1. The full list has many constants outside this range, such as e to the power pi (about 23.14) and Chaitin's constant (about 0.0079). [Wikipedia: List of mathematical constants](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/List_of_mathematical_constants). The claim is true of the short table but false for mathematical constants in general, and he does not address how constants are selected.
- **Range of physical constants.** His statement: correct. He contrasts the mathematical constants with dimensionless physical constants, which range from about 10 to the power -123 to 10 to the power 36. Standard statement: The cosmological constant in Planck units is about 10 to the power -122, and the ratio of electric to gravitational force between two protons is about 10 to the power 36. [Wikipedia: Dimensionless physical constant](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Dimensionless_physical_constant). The orders of magnitude are right.
- **Monster group as a constant.** His statement: correct. He asks whether the Monster group counts as a mathematical constant. Standard statement: The Monster is the largest sporadic finite simple group. Its order is about 8 times 10 to the power 53. [Wikipedia: Monster group](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Monster_group). He asks it as a question, and the facts behind it are right.
- **Galton board and the bell curve.** His statement: correct. He says that the bell curve is in neither the marbles nor the pegs, so it comes free from mathematics. Standard statement: The counts in the bins follow a binomial distribution. With many rows, the binomial distribution comes close to the normal distribution. [Wikipedia: Galton board](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Galton_board). The mathematics he describes is right.
- **NAND gates and truth tables.** His statement: correct. He says that once evolution has a voltage-gated ion channel, which acts like a transistor, all of logic comes free. Standard statement: The NAND gate alone is functionally complete. Every Boolean function can be built from NAND gates. [Wikipedia: Functional completeness](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Functional_completeness). The logic is right. The link between an ion channel and a transistor is an analogy.
- **Halley fractal of z cubed plus 7.** His statement: correct. He uses this fractal as a short formula that points to a rich form, which neither physics nor selection sets. Standard statement: Halley's method is a root-finding iteration of cubic order. In the complex plane, its basins of attraction have fractal edges. [Wikipedia: Halley's method](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halley%27s_method). The description is right. In one talk he says z squared plus seven and then hedges.
- **Halley fractal as a map of the halting problem.** His statement: loose. He says that the pixels that do not converge make the fractal a map of the halting problem, in a way. Standard statement: The halting problem says that no algorithm can decide, for every program and input, whether the program stops. [Wikipedia: Halting problem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Halting_problem). A pixel that does not converge within a fixed number of steps does not show undecidability, though he hedges with in a way.
- **No formula in radicals from degree six.** His statement: wrong. He says that polynomials of degree six and above have no formula in radicals. Standard statement: The Abel–Ruffini theorem says that there is no general solution in radicals for degree five and above. [Wikipedia: Abel–Ruffini theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Abel%E2%80%93Ruffini_theorem). He is off by one degree.
- **Complex numbers, quaternions and octonions.** His statement: correct. He says that these number systems keep different promises, so their algebraic laws are not set by physics. Standard statement: Normed division algebras exist only in dimensions 1, 2, 4 and 8. Quaternions lose commutativity, and octonions also lose associativity. [Baez, The Octonions](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0105155). The facts are right.
- **Octahedron and quaternions as lateral interaction.** His statement: loose. He uses the octahedron and the commutativity of quaternions as an example of patterns that interact side by side. Standard statement: Quaternion multiplication is not commutative. A link to the octahedron is not stated. [Baez, The Octonions](https://arxiv.org/abs/math/0105155). The example is not specific enough to check.
- **Liar paradox as an oscillator.** His statement: correct. He uses the liar paradox and Grim's fuzzy dynamics to show that logical objects can be active members of the space. Standard statement: Under a rule of revision the liar sentence flips between true and false at each step. In fuzzy logic, systems of self-referring sentences give attractors and chaos. [Mar and Grim 1991, Pattern and chaos](https://doi.org/10.2307/2215637). His description matches the sources.
- **Least action as minimal goal-directedness.** His statement: loose. He says that light takes the path of least action, and he uses this as the base of goal-directedness. Standard statement: Physical paths make the action stationary. The stationary point can be a minimum or a saddle, so it is not always a minimum. [Wikipedia: Stationary-action principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Stationary-action_principle). Least action is the traditional name and not the general fact. The reading as a goal is his interpretation.
- **Halting status cannot be changed by physics.** His statement: correct. He says that whether a given program halts is a fact that physics cannot change. Standard statement: Whether a given machine halts on a given input is fixed by its definition. [Turing 1936, On computable numbers](https://doi.org/10.1112/plms/s2-42.1.230). The claim is right.
- **Gödel and Turing limits on knowing a larger mind.** His statement: loose. He suggests that the limits of Gödel and Turing may stop us from proving that we are part of a larger mind. Standard statement: The first incompleteness theorem says that any consistent formal theory with enough arithmetic has true sentences that it cannot prove. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Gödel's Incompleteness Theorems](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/goedel-incompleteness/). He names no formal system, so the theorems do not apply as he states them.
- **Sphere packing in many dimensions.** His statement: correct. He uses sphere packing as a fact that is not physical. Standard statement: The densest packings are proved only in dimensions 1, 2, 3, 8 and 24. [Wikipedia: Sphere packing](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Sphere_packing). It is a valid example of a mathematical fact.
- **Symmetry groups explain particle types.** His statement: loose. He says that questions about particles end in mathematics because symmetry groups explain the particle types. Standard statement: Particle types match irreducible representations of the Poincaré group, and gauge groups fix the interactions. [Wikipedia: Wigner's classification](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wigner%27s_classification). The idea is broadly right, but he never states the specific claim.
- **Amplituhedron.** His statement: loose. He says that with the amplituhedron mathematicians touch patterns directly, and that its best explanation is not physical. Standard statement: The amplituhedron is a geometric object whose volume gives scattering amplitudes in a simplified theory (planar N = 4 super-Yang–Mills). It does not cover the Standard Model. [Arkani-Hamed and Trnka, The Amplituhedron](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.2007). The result is real but holds for a simplified theory.
- **If primes were different, cicadas would differ.** His statement: loose. He says that patterns are causes in Pearl's sense because if primes were different, cicadas would be different. Standard statement: Pearl-style counterfactuals change the value of a variable. A counterfactual about a necessary truth is a counterpossible, and standard semantics makes every counterpossible trivially true. [Baron, Colyvan and Ripley 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/philmat/nkz023). The claim needs a semantics for counterpossibles, which he does not give.
- **Game of Life glider and Turing machines.** His statement: correct. He says that a description at a higher level lets you build things, such as Turing machines from streams of gliders. Standard statement: The glider is a pattern with period 4. The Game of Life is Turing complete. [Wikipedia: Conway's Game of Life](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Conway%27s_Game_of_Life). The facts are right.
- **Turing patterns.** His statement: correct. He says that the physical side of pattern formation is well understood. Standard statement: When diffusion makes a uniform state unstable, stationary patterns appear. [Turing 1952, The chemical basis of morphogenesis](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1952.0012). The mechanism he refers to is standard.
- **Pickover biomorphs.** His statement: correct. He uses biomorphs as short formulas that point into the space of forms. Standard statement: Iterated complex maps with a changed escape test give shapes that look like organisms. [Pickover 1986, Biomorphs](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.1986.tb00317.x). His description is right.
- **Fractal inverse problem is not solvable.** His statement: loose. He says that finding the formula behind a fractal is fundamentally not solvable, as an analogy for going from anatomy back to genes. Standard statement: No general closed-form inverse is known. The problem is a hard, ill-posed search, and no proof of impossibility exists. [Wikipedia: Computational irreducibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_irreducibility). Hard and ill-posed is right, but fundamentally unsolvable is not shown.
- **Sorites paradox.** His statement: correct. He uses the sorites paradox to say that we should define continua by the tools we use to interact with them. Standard statement: Vague words together with a rule of tolerance lead to a contradiction. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Sorites Paradox](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/sorites-paradox/). His use of the paradox is standard.
- **Laplace's demon.** His statement: correct. He says that an observer who knows only the microstate cannot act or play chess. Standard statement: Laplace imagined an intellect that knows all positions and forces and so can predict everything. [Wikipedia: Laplace's demon](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Laplace%27s_demon). He describes the thought experiment correctly.
- **Geometric frustration.** His statement: correct. He uses geometric frustration as a basic form of real frustration. Standard statement: In geometric frustration, local constraints cannot all be met at the same time, as in a magnet with three spins on a triangle. [Wikipedia: Geometrical frustration](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Geometrical_frustration). The physics is right. The reading as experience is his.
- **Landauer's principle.** His statement: correct. He cites the principle and expects that it will be revised because it counts only physical cost. Standard statement: To erase one bit costs at least kT ln 2 of heat. [Landauer 1961](https://doi.org/10.1147/rd.53.0183). He cites it accurately. The revision is his conjecture.
- **Computational irreducibility.** His statement: correct. He says that there may be shortcuts that the present formalism does not capture. Standard statement: Some processes cannot be predicted faster than by running them. [Wikipedia: Computational irreducibility](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Computational_irreducibility). He uses the idea correctly.
- **Heisenberg uncertainty and reading memory.** His statement: loose. He says that you must disturb a system to read its memory, as in the uncertainty principle. Standard statement: The uncertainty principle limits how well two conjugate quantities can both be known. It is not the same as the observer effect. [Wikipedia: Uncertainty principle](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Uncertainty_principle). His analogy fits the observer effect better than the uncertainty principle.
- **Angle trisection and the round square.** His statement: correct. He suggests that some unconscious processes may be impossible in this way. Standard statement: A general angle cannot be cut into three equal parts with straightedge and compass. [Wikipedia: Angle trisection](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Angle_trisection). The mathematical fact is right.
- **Runs of 20 heads in an infinite random sequence.** His statement: correct. He uses this to say that science needs faith that laws are real. Standard statement: In an infinite sequence of fair, independent coin flips, every finite run occurs with probability 1. [Wikipedia: Infinite monkey theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Infinite_monkey_theorem). The probability fact is right.
- **Mathematical emergence.** His statement: correct. He says that if facts such as an angle sum, the sum of a series or a derivative count as emergence, then the word covers everything and decides nothing. Standard statement: The examples are standard mathematical facts. . His examples are correct.
- **Light cone and cognitive light cone.** His statement: correct. He takes the light cone of physics as the model for the axes of his cognitive light cone. Standard statement: In special relativity, the light cone of a point is the set of events that can be causally connected to it. [Wikipedia: Light cone](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Light_cone). He uses the idea correctly and calls his version a simplified cone.
- **D'Arcy Thompson's coordinate transformations.** His statement: correct. He uses them to show fields with no physical basis, and asks what the grid is. Standard statement: Smooth deformations of a grid map the form of one species onto the form of another. [D'Arcy Thompson, On Growth and Form](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1549602W). His description is right.
- **Fibonacci phyllotaxis.** His statement: correct. He uses it as a mathematical pattern in biology. Standard statement: In many plants, the counts of spirals are consecutive Fibonacci numbers. [Wikipedia: Phyllotaxis](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Phyllotaxis). The fact is right.
- **A formula true for every number except 37.** His statement: not judged. He mentions a formula that is true for every number except 37 as a strange fact of number theory. Standard statement: The formula he means cannot be identified from what he says. . There is not enough detail to check the claim.

## Formal tools in his papers {#formal-tools-in-his-papers}

- **Causal emergence.** His statement: correct. He uses it to say that a large-scale description can do more causal work than the small-scale one, which he calls a free gift from mathematics. Standard statement: Effective information is the mutual information between interventions at maximum entropy and their effects. A coarse-grained model can have more effective information than the fine-grained one. [Hoel, Albantakis and Tononi 2013](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1314922110). The papers apply the measure as defined.
- **Integrated information decomposition.** His statement: correct. His papers use it to measure causal emergence in the hidden states of learning agents and in calcium signals of xenobots. Standard statement: The method splits time-delayed mutual information into redundant, unique and synergistic parts. [Mediano et al. 2025](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2423297122). The papers use the estimator in the standard way.
- **Partial information decomposition.** His statement: correct. A paper of his uses it as the basis of the decomposition above. Standard statement: It splits the information that sources carry about a target into redundant, unique and synergistic parts. [Williams and Beer 2010](https://arxiv.org/abs/1004.2515). The paper cites the method correctly.
- **Gaussian mutual information.** His statement: correct. A paper of his estimates mutual information with a closed-form formula after it makes the data Gaussian. Standard statement: For two jointly Gaussian variables with correlation rho, the mutual information is minus one half times ln(1 - rho squared). [Cover and Thomas, Elements of Information Theory](https://doi.org/10.1002/047174882X). The formula is exact for Gaussian variables.
- **Integrated information.** His statement: correct. He uses it to look for a group-level agent in embryos and xenobots. Standard statement: Integrated information measures how much information a whole generates above its parts. [Balduzzi and Tononi 2008](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1000091). He uses it as a measure and does not make a claim about consciousness.
- **Free-energy principle and Markov blankets.** His statement: loose. His papers treat the boundaries of agents as nested Markov blankets. Standard statement: A Markov blanket separates internal states from external states in a statistical sense. Whether it marks a physical boundary of an agent is disputed. [Bruineberg et al. 2022](https://doi.org/10.1017/S0140525X21002351). The papers use the reading as a boundary and do not mention the dispute.
- **Lenia update rule.** His statement: correct. A paper of his uses Lenia to study agents that are patterns and their fear of the unknown. Standard statement: Lenia is a continuous cellular automaton with a kernel and a growth function. [Chan 2019, Lenia](https://doi.org/10.25088/ComplexSystems.28.3.251). The paper states the rule correctly.
- **Wasserstein distance on sorted profiles.** His statement: correct. A paper of his uses it to measure whether a form keeps its identity. Standard statement: In one dimension, the Wasserstein-1 distance is the L1 distance between quantile functions, and the median at each point minimizes the sum of L1 distances. [Wikipedia: Wasserstein metric](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Wasserstein_metric). The method is sound.
- **Attractors, basins and critical slowing down.** His statement: correct. He uses them to explain recovery and memory as states that a system returns to. Standard statement: These are standard ideas of nonlinear dynamics. [Strogatz, Nonlinear Dynamics and Chaos](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3907581W). He uses the ideas in the standard way.
- **Neural cellular automata.** His statement: correct. His papers use them as a model of distributed, generative growth of form. Standard statement: A neural cellular automaton is a cellular automaton whose local rule is a trained neural network. [Mordvintsev et al. 2020](https://doi.org/10.23915/distill.00023). The papers use the model correctly.
- **Diffusion models as evolutionary algorithms.** His statement: correct. A paper of his shows that a denoising step weighted by fitness acts as an evolutionary algorithm. Standard statement: Diffusion models generate data by learning to remove noise step by step. [Diffusion models are evolutionary algorithms](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02543). The result holds within the construction of the paper.
- **Symmetry breaking in diffusion.** His statement: correct. His papers say that a hierarchy of features appears as the model breaks symmetry. Standard statement: Generation in diffusion models passes through a phase transition that breaks symmetry. [Raya and Ambrogioni](https://arxiv.org/abs/2305.19693). The papers use the result correctly.
- **Johnson–Lindenstrauss random projection.** His statement: correct. A paper of his uses it to run evolution in a smaller latent space. Standard statement: A random projection to a dimension of order log n over epsilon squared keeps all pairwise distances within a factor of 1 plus or minus epsilon. [Wikipedia: Johnson–Lindenstrauss lemma](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Johnson%E2%80%93Lindenstrauss_lemma). The paper uses the lemma correctly.
- **Variational autoencoders and the bow-tie.** His statement: correct. He uses them for the genome as a compressed code and for memory as compression with creative decoding. Standard statement: An encoder, a narrow bottleneck and a decoder are trained together with the evidence lower bound. [Kingma and Welling](https://arxiv.org/abs/1312.6114). The model is right. The mapping to the genome is a hypothesis.
- **Modern Hopfield networks.** His statement: correct. A paper of his uses them for retrieval that corrects errors in an embedding space. Standard statement: The update of a continuous Hopfield network is the same as attention in a transformer. [Ramsauer et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2008.02217). The paper uses the result correctly.
- **Hyperdimensional computing.** His statement: correct. A paper of his uses it for robust storage that is spread across the whole memory. Standard statement: It binds and superposes random vectors of very high dimension. [Kanerva 2009](https://doi.org/10.1007/s12559-009-9009-8). The paper uses the method correctly.
- **Self-organized criticality.** His statement: correct. His papers use it as a set point for remapping. Standard statement: Systems that are driven slowly can tune themselves to a critical state with power laws. [Bak, Tang and Wiesenfeld 1987](https://doi.org/10.1103/PhysRevLett.59.381). The papers use the idea correctly.
- **Sheaves and contextuality.** His statement: loose. A paper of his treats a failure of coherence as context dependence that is not classical. Standard statement: Contextuality means that local sections do not glue to a global section. [Abramsky and Brandenburger 2011](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/13/11/113036). The paper asserts the identification and does not derive it.
- **Universal properties as a virtual governor.** His statement: loose. A paper of his uses universal properties as a mathematical example of a virtual governor. Standard statement: A universal property fixes an object, up to unique isomorphism, by its relations to all other objects. [Riehl, Category Theory in Context](https://emilyriehl.github.io/files/context.pdf). The definition is right, but the governor is an analogy.
- **Arrow's impossibility theorem.** His statement: loose. A paper of his uses it to describe the governor as a dictator that emerges. Standard statement: With three or more options, no voting rule meets all of these conditions: unrestricted domain, Pareto, independence of irrelevant alternatives and no dictator. [Wikipedia: Arrow's impossibility theorem](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arrow%27s_impossibility_theorem). The paper does not check that the conditions hold for the biological case.
- **Reservoir computing.** His statement: correct. A paper of his builds an agent from a frozen dynamical system with a trained readout. Standard statement: Reservoir computing uses a fixed dynamical system and trains only the readout. [Lukoševičius and Jaeger 2009](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cosrev.2009.03.005). The paper uses the method correctly.
- **Magic square and tic-tac-toe.** His statement: correct. A paper of his uses it to show two representations of one game. Standard statement: Picking three numbers that sum to 15 from 1 to 9 is the same game as tic-tac-toe on the 3 by 3 magic square. [Wikipedia: Number Scrabble](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Number_Scrabble). The equivalence is right.
- **Word2vec offsets.** His statement: correct. A paper of his uses king minus man plus woman, which comes close to queen, to show geometry of meaning. Standard statement: In word2vec embeddings, some relations between words appear as constant vector offsets. [Mikolov et al. 2013](https://arxiv.org/abs/1301.3781). The example is standard.
- **Sorting algorithms as minimal agents.** His statement: correct. A paper of his finds competencies that nobody programmed, such as delayed gratification, in simple sorting algorithms. Standard statement: Bubble sort swaps neighbours that are out of order until the list is sorted. The paper defines its own measures. [Classical sorting algorithms as a model of morphogenesis](https://doi.org/10.1177/10597123241269740). The results hold as the paper defines them.
- **Gene networks that learn.** His statement: correct. He says that gene-network equations show six kinds of learning, which he calls a free gift from the mathematics of networks. Standard statement: A published study trained models of gene regulatory networks and found several kinds of learning in them. [Biswas et al. 2021](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.isci.2021.102131). The gene-network result is published. The result for predator–prey equations is not yet published.
- **Random Boolean networks.** His statement: correct. He uses them to say that you get out more than you put in. Standard statement: Random networks of Boolean genes show ordered behaviour, especially near a critical point. [Kauffman 1969](https://doi.org/10.1016/0022-5193(69)90015-0). He uses the result correctly.
- **Platonic representation hypothesis.** His statement: loose. He uses it to say that machine-learning models converge on the same ideas. Standard statement: The hypothesis says that representations in different models converge toward one shared statistical model of reality. [Huh et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987). The hypothesis is about learned representations and does not show ingression.
- **t-SNE with five clusters.** His statement: loose. He reads five clusters in a t-SNE plot as five discrete types of training effect. Standard statement: t-SNE keeps local neighbours, but the number and spacing of clusters can depend on its settings. [Wikipedia: t-distributed stochastic neighbor embedding](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/T-distributed_stochastic_neighbor_embedding). To show that the types are discrete needs a check beyond t-SNE.
- **Simulated annealing and cognitive temperature.** His statement: correct. He says that stress raises the temperature of a system so that it can reach new states. Standard statement: Simulated annealing accepts worse moves with a probability that falls as the temperature is lowered. [Kirkpatrick et al. 1983](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.220.4598.671). He uses it correctly as an analogy.

## Philosophy of mathematics he engages {#philosophy}

- **Mathematical Platonism.** It is his core claim, that mathematical patterns exist apart from the physical world. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Platonism in the Philosophy of Mathematics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/platonism-mathematics/); [Panza and Sereni, Plato's Problem](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19976404W)
- **Popper's World 3 and interactionism.** His model of a non-physical mind extends this older model. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Karl Popper](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/popper/); [Popper and Eccles, The Self and Its Brain](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1984578W)
- **Nominalism and fictionalism.** Critics use these views to deny that mathematical objects exist, which removes the need for his space. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Nominalism in the Philosophy of Mathematics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/nominalism-mathematics/); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Fictionalism in the Philosophy of Mathematics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/fictionalism-mathematics/); [Field, Science Without Numbers](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4086247W)
- **Structuralism and structural realism.** Guests use these views to say that relations and real patterns can do the work of his forms. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Structuralism in the Philosophy of Mathematics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/structuralism-mathematics/); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Structural Realism](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/structural-realism/); [Shapiro, Thinking about Mathematics](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3290275W)
- **Indispensability.** This argument is the standard route from the use of mathematics in science to belief in mathematical objects. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Indispensability Arguments in the Philosophy of Mathematics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mathphil-indis/); [Colyvan, The Indispensability of Mathematics](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL8003839W)
- **Mathematical explanation in science.** His cicada argument depends on the claim that mathematics can explain a biological fact. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Mathematical Explanation](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/mathematics-explanation/); [Baker 2005](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/fzi223); [Lange, Because Without Cause](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21137402W); [Colyvan, An Introduction to the Philosophy of Mathematics](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL16564317W)
- **Counterpossibles.** His claim that cicadas would differ if primes differed needs a theory of counterfactuals about impossible cases. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Impossible Worlds](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/impossible-worlds/); [Baron, Colyvan and Ripley 2020](https://doi.org/10.1093/philmat/nkz023)
- **Embodied and humanist mathematics.** These views say that mathematics comes from human bodies and culture, against his view of a space that exists first. [Lakoff and Núñez, Where Mathematics Comes From](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL14957957W); [Davis and Hersh, The Mathematical Experience](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2907624W)
- **Whitehead: eternal objects and ingression.** His word ingression comes from Whitehead, so the original meaning shows how far he departs from it. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Alfred North Whitehead](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/whitehead/); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Process Philosophy](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/process-philosophy/); [Whitehead, Process and Reality](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1133700W)
- **Evolving forms.** He accepts that forms can evolve and that the space is not fixed, which changes what Platonism means for him. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Charles Sanders Peirce](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/peirce/); [Kauffman and Roli 2021](https://doi.org/10.3390/e23111467)
- **Deleuze's inversion of Platonism.** Process philosophers raise it as an alternative in which forms come from becoming. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Gilles Deleuze](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/deleuze/)
- **Cognitive Platonism.** It is the closest rival view, and he says that he agrees with most of it. [Dodig-Crnkovic 2025](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202512.0033.v1)
- **Abstract objects in general.** It gives the background for his claim that patterns are not physical but still cause things. [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Abstract Objects](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/abstract-objects/); [Stanford Encyclopedia of Philosophy: Platonism in Metaphysics](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/platonism/)
