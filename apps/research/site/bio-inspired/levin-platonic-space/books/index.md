# Books

Part of the field guide [Michael Levin on Platonic space, patterns and mathematics](/bio-inspired/levin-platonic-space/).

These are the books that Michael Levin cites or recommends in his talks, blog posts and papers. The groups start with the books closest to his argument about Platonic space and patterns. Each book has a short note on what he uses it for and up to two places where he uses it.

## Ten to read first {#ten-to-read-first}

1. [Gödel, Escher, Bach: An Eternal Golden Braid](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL716850W), Douglas Hofstadter. He recommends it to prepare the mind to break the line between data and rules, and as a model of self-reference that needs no magic.
2. [The Origins of Order: Self-Organization and Selection in Evolution](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3493377W), Stuart Kauffman. He uses it for self-organization and for the idea of a structured space of possibilities that evolution explores.
3. [Arrival of the Fittest: Solving Evolution's Greatest Puzzle](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL25857644W), Andreas Wagner. He uses it to ask where the useful novelty in evolution comes from, and as a source of free gifts that evolution does not pay for.
4. [On Growth and Form](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1549602W), D'Arcy W. Thompson. He uses its coordinate transformations to show mathematical forms in biology and to ask what the underlying grid is.
5. [Process and Reality: An Essay in Cosmology](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1133700W), Alfred North Whitehead. It is the source of his word ingression, which he says he uses in a different sense.
6. [The Self and Its Brain: An Argument for Interactionism](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1984578W), Karl Popper and John Eccles. He presents his model as an extension of this earlier interactionist model of a non-physical mind.
7. [I Am a Strange Loop](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL8067922W), Douglas Hofstadter. He uses it for patterns that make themselves real and for agents that split and merge as a minimal strange loop.
8. [Because Without Cause: Non-Causal Explanations in Science and Mathematics](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21137402W), Marc Lange. He cites it for mathematical explanations of physical and biological facts that do not use causes.
9. [Plato's Problem: An Introduction to Mathematical Platonism](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL19976404W), Marco Panza and Andrea Sereni. He cites it as background on mathematical Platonism and on whether mathematics is discovered or invented.
10. [The Emperor's New Mind](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL43191626W), Roger Penrose. He cites Penrose as a physicist who is a Platonist, and says that his own view does not depend on such physicists.

## Core to Platonic space and patterns {#core-to-platonic-space-and-patterns}

- [Arrival of the Fittest: Solving Evolution's Greatest Puzzle](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL25857644W), Andreas Wagner, 2014. On his Inspiration list. He uses it to ask where the useful novelty in evolution comes from, and as a source of free gifts that evolution does not pay for.
- [On Growth and Form](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1549602W), D'Arcy W. Thompson, 1917. On his Inspiration list. He uses its coordinate transformations to show mathematical forms in biology and to ask what the underlying grid is.

- [The Self and Its Brain: An Argument for Interactionism](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1984578W), Karl Popper and John Eccles, 1977. He presents his model as an extension of this earlier interactionist model of a non-physical mind.
- [I Am a Strange Loop](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL8067922W), Douglas Hofstadter, 2007. He uses it for patterns that make themselves real and for agents that split and merge as a minimal strange loop.

- [At Home in the Universe](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL8003172W), Stuart Kauffman, 1995. On his Inspiration list. Its idea of order for free inspired him in the 1980s, and he says that the competencies he studies go further than order.
- [Vehicles: Experiments in Synthetic Psychology](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1811686W), Valentino Braitenberg, 1984. He calls it a work of genius but rejects its claim that the vehicles contain nothing that the builder did not put in.

- [The Origins of Evolutionary Innovations](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL16184812W), Andreas Wagner, 2011. On his Inspiration list. He uses it for the origin of innovation and for novelty that does not come from adaptation.
- [Investigations](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3493380W), Stuart Kauffman, 2000. He cites it for the idea of the adjacent possible.
- [A World Beyond Physics](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL20695336W), Stuart Kauffman, 2019. He cites it for the adjacent possible and for self-organization.
- [Collected Papers](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1834319W), C. S. Peirce, 1931. He uses Peirce to support the idea that Platonic forms can themselves evolve.
- [Our Mathematical Universe](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL17785472W), Max Tegmark, 2014. He cites it as an example of a physicist who holds that the world is mathematical.
- [The Fabric of Reality](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2635835W), David Deutsch, 1997. He cites it as one version of the Platonist view among physicists.
- [Shadows of the Mind](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3474171W), Roger Penrose, 1994. He cites it with The Emperor's New Mind as a Platonist precedent from physics.
- [The Road to Reality](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3474173W), Roger Penrose, 2004. He cites it with the other Penrose books as a Platonist precedent from physics.
- [Fearful Symmetry: The Search for Beauty in Modern Physics](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL485891W), A. Zee, 1986. He uses it to say that the patterns of physics come from symmetry, so that questions in physics end in mathematics.
- [Causality: Models, Reasoning, and Inference](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL4624596W), Judea Pearl, 2000. He uses its counterfactual test of causation to argue that mathematical facts can be causes, for example in the cicada case.
- [A New Science of Life: The Hypothesis of Formative Causation](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2042887W), Rupert Sheldrake, 1981. He cites it for non-physical forms in anatomy, says that his own view is wider, and denies that bioelectric fields are Sheldrake's fields.
- [Matter and Memory](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL39682147W), Henri Bergson, 1896. He cites it for patterns that go beyond one person and that act on matter.
- [Creative Evolution](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL8273960W), Henri Bergson, 1907. He cites it with Matter and Memory for patterns that act on matter.
- [Atom and Archetype: The Pauli/Jung Letters 1932–1958](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL1247789W), Wolfgang Pauli and C. G. Jung, 2001. He cites it because Pauli could not exclude direction in natural law.
- [The Interpretation of Nature and the Psyche](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL26760035W), C. G. Jung and W. Pauli, 1955. He cites it for synchronicity and for Jung's primordial images.
- [Incomplete Nature: How Mind Emerged from Matter](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL16679855W), Terrence Deacon, 2011. He cites it for absences and constraints that act as causes from the top down.
- [Context Changes Everything: How Constraints Create Coherence](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL29175233W), Alicia Juarrero, 2023. He cites it where he discusses constraints as causes. ◇
- [The Geometry of Evolution: Adaptive Landscapes and Theoretical Morphospaces](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL13668974W), George McGhee, 2007. He cites it where he discusses morphospace, the space of possible forms. ◇
- [Fractal Physiology and Chaos in Medicine](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL2921769W), Bruce J. West, 1990. He cites it for fractal patterns in the body and its functions.
- [Chaos and Fractals](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL15800589W), H.-O. Peitgen, H. Jürgens and D. Saupe, 1992. He cites it for mathematics in biology.
- [The Case Against Reality](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL21763332W), Donald Hoffman, 2019. He uses Hoffman's word interface as a synonym for his own word pointer, and later says that his view does not rely on Hoffman.
- [Chance and Necessity](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL12860347W), Jacques Monod, 1970. He cites it in his discussion of necessity and freedom.
- [How the Self Controls Its Brain](https://openlibrary.org/works/OL3961095W), John Eccles, 1994. He cites it with The Self and Its Brain for interactionism.
- Principles of Analytical Biology and the Theory of Cellular Fields, A. G. Gurwitsch, 1991. He cites it for non-physical fields that shape anatomy.

## Papers by others that he relies on {#papers-by-others}

- [The Platonic Representation Hypothesis](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987), Huh, Cheung, Wang, Isola, 2024. He wants to join this finding, that machine-learning models converge on shared representations, with his own view. It is about learned representations and does not show ingression. ◇
- [The unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics in the natural sciences](https://doi.org/10.1002/cpa.3160130102), Wigner, 1960. He uses it as the template for his phrase about the unreasonable effectiveness of behavioral science in development.
- [The unreasonable effectiveness of mathematics](https://doi.org/10.1080/00029890.1980.11994966), Hamming, 1980. He cites it for the same question asked by an engineer.
- [A commentary on prime numbers and life cycles of periodical cicadas](https://doi.org/10.1086/286158), Cox and Carlton, 1998. It supports his main example of a mathematical cause in biology.
- [Are there genuine mathematical explanations of physical phenomena?](https://doi.org/10.1093/mind/fzi223), Baker, 2005. It brings the cicada case into the philosophy of mathematical explanation.
- [Mathematical explanation in science](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axp025), Baker, 2009. He cites it with Baker 2005 for mathematical explanation.
- [Mathematical explanation](https://doi.org/10.1007/BF00354494), Steiner, 1978. He cites it as the classic account of proofs that explain.
- [Are there non-causal explanations (of particular events)?](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axs047), Skow, 2014. He cites it for explanations that do not use causes.
- [Pattern and chaos: new images in the semantics of paradox](https://doi.org/10.2307/2215637), Mar and Grim, 1991. It treats the liar paradox as a dynamical system, which he uses as an active pattern in the space.
- [Self-reference and chaos in fuzzy logic](https://doi.org/10.1109/91.251925), Grim, 1993. He cites it with Mar and Grim for logical patterns that change in time.
- [Gene networks and liar paradoxes](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.200900072), Isalan, 2009. He cites it for logical structures that appear in gene networks.
- [Biomorphs: computer displays of biological forms generated from mathematical feedback loops](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1467-8659.1986.tb00317.x), Pickover, 1986. He uses it for short formulas that give forms that look alive.
- [Inherency of form and function in animal development and evolution](https://doi.org/10.3389/fphys.2019.00702), Newman, 2019. He cites it for forms that tissue gets free from physics and mathematics.
- [Quantifying causal emergence shows that macro can beat micro](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1314922110), Hoel, Albantakis, Tononi, 2013. He uses it as a measure of causation from the top down and as part of his argument that mathematics gives free gifts.
- [When the map is better than the territory](https://doi.org/10.3390/e19050188), Hoel, 2017. He cites it with Hoel 2013 for causal emergence.
- [Reconciling emergences](https://doi.org/10.1371/journal.pcbi.1008289), Rosas et al., 2020. His lab uses its measure of causal emergence.
- [Toward a unified taxonomy of information dynamics via Integrated Information Decomposition](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.2423297122), Mediano et al., 2025. His lab uses its method to split information into redundant, unique and synergistic parts.
- [The mathematical universe](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10701-007-9186-9), Tegmark, 2008. He cites it as the strongest Platonist view from a physicist.
- [The physicalization of metamathematics](https://arxiv.org/abs/2204.05123), Wolfram, 2022. He cites it for mathematics and physics that come from one substrate.
- [The world is not a theorem](https://doi.org/10.3390/e23111467), Kauffman and Roli, 2021. He cites it for possibilities that cannot be listed in advance, and he accepts that Platonic space is not fixed.
- [The chemical basis of morphogenesis](https://doi.org/10.1098/rstb.1952.0012), Turing, 1952. He uses it to show a symmetry between the origin of bodies and the origin of minds.
- [Behavior, purpose and teleology](https://doi.org/10.1086/286788), Rosenblueth, Wiener, Bigelow, 1943. He uses it for a continuous scale of goal-directedness.
- [The cognitive domain of a glider in the Game of Life](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00125), Beer, 2014. He uses it to show that a pattern can have cognition.
- [How downwards causation occurs in digital computers](https://doi.org/10.1007/s10701-019-00307-6), Ellis and Drossel, 2019. He cites it for causation from the top down that needs no magic.
- [A theory of biological relativity](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2011.0067), Noble, 2012. He cites it for the idea that no level of causation has priority.
- [The interface theory of perception](https://doi.org/10.3758/s13423-015-0890-8), Hoffman, Singh, Prakash, 2015. He compares its word interface to his own word pointer.
- [Irruption theory](https://doi.org/10.3390/e25050748), Froese, 2023. He names it as a model that is not physicalist and that he wants to join with his own.
- [The sheaf-theoretic structure of non-locality and contextuality](https://doi.org/10.1088/1367-2630/13/11/113036), Abramsky and Brandenburger, 2011. A paper of his uses it to treat a failure of coherence as contextuality.
- [Harnessing the universal geometry of embeddings](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540), Jha, Zhang, Shmatikov, Morris, 2025. A paper of his cites it for a geometry that different embedding models share.
- [The genomic code: the genome instantiates a generative model of the organism](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tig.2025.01.008), Mitchell and Cheney, 2025. His papers cite it for the genome as a compressed code for the body.
- [How can evolution learn?](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tree.2015.11.009), Watson and Szathmáry, 2016. His papers cite it for evolution as a learning process.
- [The common patterns of nature](https://doi.org/10.1111/j.1420-9101.2009.01775.x), Frank, 2009. He cites it. Its thesis is that common patterns follow from maximum-entropy constraints. ◇
- [Platonic Space as Cognitive Construct](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202512.0033.v1), Dodig-Crnkovic, 2025. It is the closest rival view. He agrees with most of it but differs on constraint against enablement.
