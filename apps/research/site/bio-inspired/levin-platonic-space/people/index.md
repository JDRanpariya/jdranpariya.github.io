# People and labs

Part of the field guide [Michael Levin on Platonic space, patterns and mathematics](/bio-inspired/levin-platonic-space/).

These are the people and labs around Michael Levin's work on Platonic space and patterns. The first group gave talks at his Platonic Space symposium or discussed the topic with him. The second group is the labs that he lists as of interest, with their key works and any link to him.

## Closest to this theme {#closest}

1. [Chris Fields](https://chrisfieldsresearch.com/). He gave the symposium talk From Experience to Math, and Levin sends critics of his physics claims to him.
2. [Karl Friston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_J._Friston). He gave a symposium talk on the Platonic nature of things, and Levin discusses Platonic forms in Friston's mathematics.
3. [Tom Froese](https://www.oist.jp/research/tom-froese). His symposium talk reads Platonic forms as informational operators at the interface between mind and body.
4. [Juan P. Aguilera](https://juan.ag/). His talk gives a modern Platonist view of mathematics after Gödel and set theory.
5. [Matt Segall](https://footnotes2plato.com/about-me/). His talk reads Levin's Platonic programme through Whitehead's process philosophy and ingression.
6. [Timothy N. W. Jackson](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=vOnot8oAAAAJ&hl=en). His talk offers an alternative to fixed Platonism in which forms and logic themselves develop.
7. [Gordana Dodig-Crnkovic](https://www.gordana.se/main.html). Her cognitive Platonism is the closest rival view, and Levin says he agrees with most of it.
8. [Brian Cheung](https://briancheung.github.io/). His talk shows that representations converge across brains, models and modalities.
9. [Pavel Chvykov](https://www.pchvykov.com/). His talk explains with statistical physics why physical systems settle into ordered patterns.
10. [Donald D. Hoffman](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~ddhoff/). Levin says that physical objects are pointers, or in Hoffman's word interfaces, to the space of patterns, and later says that his view does not depend on Hoffman.
11. [Giuseppe Longo](https://www.di.ens.fr/users/longo/). He denies a fixed space of possibilities for life, which is a direct test for a Platonic space. ◇
12. [Howard H. Pattee](https://www.binghamton.edu/ssie/people/howard-pattee.html). The cut between symbol and matter is the closest older framing of how descriptions that are not physical constrain physical systems. ◇
13. [Patrick Grim](https://pgrim.org/). Levin uses Grim's dynamic logical patterns as an example of active contents of Platonic space.

## Platonic Space symposium and conversations {#platonic-space-symposium-and-conversations}

- [Chris Fields](https://chrisfieldsresearch.com/), Allen Discovery Center, Tufts University. He applies quantum information theory and the free-energy principle to physics, biology and cognitive science. Most of his recent biology work is with Levin. He is Levin's closest co-author, with more than 35 joint papers, and he gave the symposium talk From Experience to Math. Key works: [Competency in navigating arbitrary spaces as an invariant for analyzing cognition in diverse embodiments (2022)](https://doi.org/10.3390/e24060819); [A free energy principle for generic quantum systems (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2022.05.006); [Thoughts and thinkers: on the complementarity between objects and processes (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2025.01.008); [Scale-free biology (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.201900228).
- [Karl Friston](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Karl_J._Friston), University College London. He is the main architect of the free-energy principle and active inference. In brain imaging he is known for statistical parametric mapping. He co-wrote about 10 papers with Levin and gave the symposium talk On the (Platonic) Nature of Things. Key works: [The free-energy principle: a unified brain theory? (2010)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nrn2787); [Knowing one's place: a free-energy approach to pattern regulation (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsif.2014.1383); [Morphogenesis as Bayesian inference (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.plrev.2019.06.001).
- [Tom Froese](https://www.oist.jp/research/tom-froese), Okinawa Institute of Science and Technology. He develops the enactive approach through irruption theory. This theory says that experience cannot be reduced to physiology, so it shows up as unexplained variability. He gave a symposium talk, had conversations on Levin's channel, and co-wrote the 2026 paper Defining Life. Key works: [Enactive artificial intelligence (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.artint.2008.12.001); [Irruption theory: a novel conceptualization of the enactive account of motivated activity (2023)](https://doi.org/10.3390/e25050748).
- [Joel Dietz](https://connection.mit.edu/people/joel-dietz/), MIT Connection Science. He is an entrepreneur and intellectual historian who helped found Ethereum and MetaMask. His symposium talk traces the history of morphospace and proposes ways to make Platonic claims testable. He gave a talk at the symposium.
- [Alexey Tolchinsky](https://alexeytolchinsky.com/about), George Washington University. He is a clinical psychologist who works on trauma and complex psychotherapy. He joins affective neuroscience, chaos theory and active inference with psychodynamic therapy. He co-wrote two papers with Levin, gave a symposium talk and wrote a guest post on his blog. Key works: [Narrative fallacy and other limitations of psychodynamic case formulation (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1037/pri0000231); [A case for chaos theory inclusion in neuropsychoanalytic modeling (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1080/15294145.2023.2191983); [Acute trauma in adulthood in the context of childhood traumatic experiences (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1080/15294145.2014.963645).
- [Benjamin Lyons](https://interestingessays.substack.com/), Independent researcher. He writes on economics, neuroscience, biology and mathematics. With Levin he models prices as a glue that holds collective intelligence together. He co-wrote three papers with Levin, including Alignment is to a virtual governor, and gave a symposium talk. Key works: [Cognitive glues are shared models of relative scarcities: the economics of collective intelligence (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsta.2024.0528); [The Definition of the Cartesian Product Is Weird (2024)](https://benjaminlyons9.wordpress.com/wp-content/uploads/2024/06/definition-cartesian-product-weird.pdf); [Is-ought in math (2025)](https://interestingessays.substack.com/p/is-ought-in-math).
- [Olaf Witkowski](https://olafwitkowski.com/), Cognisee and Nooma Labs. He works on artificial life, collective intelligence of machines and hybrid human-machine cognition. He also writes on the ethics of artificial beings. He co-wrote several papers with Levin, is writing a book with him, and gave a symposium talk. Key works: [Hybrid Life: Integrating biological, artificial, and cognitive systems (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1002/wcs.1662); [The Ethics of Life as It Could Be: Do We Have Moral Obligations to Artificial Life? (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1162/artl_a_00436); [Toward an ethics of autopoietic technology: Stress, care, and intelligence (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.biosystems.2023.104964).
- [Mariana Emauz Valdetaro](https://github.com/mvtta), Independent researcher, SEMF. She collects data on the speed of bioelectric and chemical waves in tissues and plants. Her symposium paper asks whether mathematical patterns act as animated patterns in physical systems. She gave a talk at the symposium. Key works: [Is Mathematics Animated? (2025)](https://thoughtforms.life/wp-content/uploads/2025/08/IS-MATHEMATICS-ANIMATED_.docx).
- [Lucy Spouncer](https://sites.google.com/site/psafronov/), University of Edinburgh. She is a doctoral student in homotopy theory, category theory and algebraic geometry. Her symposium talk treats ingression as translation between systems of presentation. She gave a talk at the symposium.
- [Lauren N. Ross](https://sites.socsci.uci.edu/~rossl/), University of California, Irvine. She works on explanation and causation in the life sciences. She studies types of causal structure and why some causes are picked as explanations. She gave a symposium talk and had a conversation on his channel, and Levin says a joint paper is coming. Key works: [Explanation in Biology (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009300940); [Causal Concepts in Biology: How Pathways Differ from Mechanisms and Why It Matters (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1093/bjps/axy078); [Causal Selection and the Pathway Concept (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1086/699022); [Causal Approaches to Scientific Explanation (2023)](https://plato.stanford.edu/entries/causal-explanation-science/).
- [Akarsh Kumar](https://akarshkumar.com/), MIT CSAIL. He applies ideas from evolution and artificial life to AI, such as open-ended search and self-play. His symposium talk argues that gradient descent can give tangled representations. He gave a symposium talk and a second talk on Levin's channel. Key works: [Questioning Representational Optimism in Deep Learning: The Fractured Entangled Representation Hypothesis (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.11581); [Automating the Search for Artificial Life with Foundation Models (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2412.17799); [Digital Red Queen: Adversarial Program Evolution in Core War with LLMs (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2601.03335).
- [Juan P. Aguilera](https://juan.ag/), TU Wien. He works in mathematical logic, such as set theory, infinite games, large cardinals and proof theory. With colleagues he defined new large cardinals. He gave a talk at the symposium. Key works: [Large cardinals, structural reflection, and the HOD Conjecture (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.11568); [Large cardinals beyond HOD (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.10254); [The metamathematics of separated determinacy (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00222-025-01322-3); [The consistency strength of long projective determinacy (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1017/jsl.2019.78).
- [Blaise Agüera y Arcas](https://research.google/people/blaise-aguera-y-arcas/), Google. He leads basic research at Google on neural computing, active inference, evolution and artificial life. His work shows self-replicating programs that arise from random programs. He co-wrote the 2026 paper What Lives? with Levin, did a podcast with him and gave a symposium talk. Key works: [What Is Intelligence? Lessons from AI About Evolution, Computing, and Minds (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1162/anti.5czb); [Computational Life: How Well-formed, Self-replicating Programs Emerge from Simple Interaction (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2406.19108); [What is the future of intelligence? The answer could lie in the story of its evolution (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1038/d41586-025-03857-0).
- [Matt Segall](https://footnotes2plato.com/about-me/), California Institute of Integral Studies. He works on process philosophy, mainly Whitehead. He has written on Whitehead's eternal objects, the forms that ingress into events. He co-edits the book that includes Levin's Platonic-space chapter, did a podcast with him and gave a symposium talk. Key works: [Standing Firm in the Flux: On Whitehead's Eternal Objects (2023)](https://doi.org/10.5406/21543682.52.2.02); [Physics Within the Bounds of Feeling Alone (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1080/02604027.2024.2406182); [Physics of the World-Soul: Alfred North Whitehead's Adventure in Cosmology (2021)](https://footnotes2plato.com/about-me/).
- [Iain McGilchrist](https://channelmcgilchrist.com/about/), Ralston College. He is a psychiatrist and writer on the two halves of the brain. He argues that they attend to the world in different ways, and that this shapes culture and knowledge. He had several conversations with Levin, commented on his Platonic-space paper and gave a symposium talk. Key works: [The Matter with Things: Our Brains, Our Delusions, and the Unmaking of the World (2021)](https://channelmcgilchrist.com/matter-with-things/); [The Master and His Emissary: The Divided Brain and the Making of the Western World (2009)](https://doi.org/10.12987/9780300247459).
- [Denis Noble](https://www.acadeuro.org/ae/User/Noble_Denis), University of Oxford. He built the first mathematical model of the heart pacemaker and works in systems physiology. He argues that no level of causation in biology has priority. His book is on Levin's reading list, he gave a symposium talk, and Levin wrote a chapter in a book that he co-edited. Key works: [Mathematics and biology: The ultimate interface? (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.pbiomolbio.2015.09.002); [A theory of biological relativity: no privileged level of causation (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1098/rsfs.2011.0067); [Dance to the Tune of Life: Biological Relativity (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781316771488); [Understanding Living Systems (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781009277396).
- [Rishi Jha](https://www.cs.cornell.edu/index.php/people/rishi-jha-0), Cornell University. He works on the security of machine learning. With Morris he showed that embeddings from different models can be translated without paired data. He gave a symposium talk with Jack Morris. Key works: [Harnessing the Universal Geometry of Embeddings (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540); [Platonic Representations in the Human Brain: Unsupervised Recovery of Universal Geometry (2026)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.20496); [Adversarial Illusions in Multi-Modal Embeddings (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11804); [Multi-Agent Systems Execute Arbitrary Malicious Code (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.12188).
- [Jack Morris](https://jxmo.io/), Cornell University (PhD). He works on language models, text embeddings and information theory. His work includes turning text embeddings back into text. He gave a symposium talk with Rishi Jha. Key works: [Harnessing the Universal Geometry of Embeddings (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.12540); [Text Embeddings Reveal (Almost) As Much As Text (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.06816); [Contextual Document Embeddings (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.02525); [How much do language models memorize? (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2505.24832).
- [Sam A. Senchal](https://github.com/SASenchal/Observer-Theory-Extension), Independent. He extends Wolfram's observer theory with category theory. Observers sample the space of all computations and build nested domains of reality. He gave a talk at the symposium. Key works: [Observer Theory and the Ruliad: An Extension to the Wolfram Model (2025)](https://wolframinstitute.org/output/observer-theory-and-the-ruliad-an-extension-to-the-wolfram-model).
- [Darren Iammarino](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=YNrxRaYAAAAJ&hl=en), University of San Diego. He works on religion and science and on process theology. His recent papers argue for a set of universals that grows. He gave a symposium talk and had a recorded conversation with Levin. Key works: [Not-So-Eternal Objects: The Case for an Expanding Set of Universals and a Refocused Ontological Principle (2025)](https://doi.org/10.5406/21543682.54.1.02); [God is a Boltzmann Brane: Arriving at God via Physicalism (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1080/14746700.2024.2436786).
- [Timothy N. W. Jackson](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=vOnot8oAAAAJ&hl=en), University of Melbourne. His lab work is on the evolution of venom. He also writes philosophy of evolution in which stable forms come from variation. He co-wrote the 2026 paper Defining Life, wrote a guest post on Levin's blog, had several conversations with him and gave a symposium talk. Key works: [Causes and Consequences of Snake Venom Variation (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.tips.2020.05.006); [The structural and functional diversification of the Toxicofera reptile venom system (2012)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.toxicon.2012.02.013); [Excess and ontogenesis (2025)](https://timothyjackson.substack.com/p/excess-and-ontogenesis).
- [Giulio Ruffini](https://giulioruffini.github.io/), Neuroelectrics, Barcelona. He develops a theory of consciousness and agents based on algorithmic information and compression. He also builds brain models for brain stimulation and for disorders such as epilepsy. He co-wrote a 2026 preprint with Levin and gave a symposium talk. Key works: [The Algorithmic Regulator (2026)](https://doi.org/10.3390/e28030257); [An algorithmic information theory of consciousness (2017)](https://doi.org/10.1093/nc/nix019); [Structured Dynamics in the Algorithmic Agent (2025)](https://doi.org/10.3390/e27010090); [Rosetta stone of neural mass models (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.physrep.2026.05.004).
- [Elliot Murphy](https://scholar.google.co.uk/citations?user=4AYNRj0AAAAJ&hl=en), University of Texas Health Science Center at Houston. He studies the brain basis of language with recordings from inside the brain. He proposes models of syntax based on brain oscillations. He gave a symposium talk and had a discussion with Levin on his channel. Key works: [The nature of language and the structure of reality (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1515/9783111293776-008); [The Oscillatory Nature of Language (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1017/9781108864466); [ROSE: A Universal Neural Grammar (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1080/17588928.2025.2523875); [The brain dynamics of linguistic computation (2015)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fpsyg.2015.01515).
- [David B. Resnik](https://www.niehs.nih.gov/research/resources/bioethics/bioethicist), National Institute of Environmental Health Sciences, NIH. He works on research ethics, such as misconduct, authorship and the use of AI in science. With Levin he wrote a framework for goal-directedness in biology. He co-wrote the two-part Mind Everywhere paper with Levin and gave a symposium talk. Key works: [Mind Everywhere: A Framework for Conceptualizing Goal-Directedness in Biology and Other Domains, Part One (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s13752-025-00523-6); [The Ethics of Science (2005)](https://doi.org/10.4324/9780203979068); [Responsible Conduct of Research (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1093/oso/9780197547090.001.0001).
- [Gordana Dodig-Crnkovic](https://www.gordana.se/main.html), Mälardalen University and Chalmers University of Technology. She works on an info-computational philosophy of nature and on cognition across living systems. Her current work develops cognitive Platonism, where forms are constraints that cognition builds. She co-wrote the 2026 paper Defining Life and gave a symposium talk, and Levin says he agrees with most of her preprint. Key works: [Platonic Space as Cognitive Construct: Toward a Framework of Cognitive Platonism (2025)](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202512.0033.v1); [Cognitive Platonism II: From Natural Regularities to Abstract Forms (2026)](https://doi.org/10.20944/preprints202608.2158.v1); [Alan Turing's Legacy: Info-computational Philosophy of Nature (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1007/978-3-642-37225-4_6).
- [Brian Cheung](https://briancheung.github.io/), University of California, San Francisco. He studies how intelligence emerges in natural, artificial and collective systems. His main line is that representations of different models become more alike as the models improve. He gave a talk at the symposium. Key works: [The Platonic Representation Hypothesis (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2405.07987); [Words That Make Language Models Perceive (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2510.02425); [What if eye...? Computationally recreating vision evolution (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1126/sciadv.ady2888).
- [Douglas E. Brash](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=QHCv7ZIAAAAJ&hl=en), Yale School of Medicine. His lab work is on UV light, p53 mutations and the origin of skin cancer. He also writes on causation in biology as outcomes shaped by constraints. He co-wrote two papers with Levin, had working sessions with him and gave a symposium talk. Key works: [Rethinking Causation for Data-intensive Biology: Constraints, Cancellations, and Quantized Organisms (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1002/bies.201900135); [A call for a better understanding of causation in cell biology (2019)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41580-019-0127-1); [A role for sunlight in skin cancer: UV-induced p53 mutations in squamous cell carcinoma (1991)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.88.22.10124); [Sunburn and p53 in the onset of skin cancer (1994)](https://doi.org/10.1038/372773a0).
- [Pavel Chvykov](https://www.pchvykov.com/), IGDORE. He uses statistical physics, dynamical systems and robot swarms to study self-organization. His low-rattling principle predicts which states a driven, noisy system settles into. He gave a symposium talk and had a recorded conversation on Levin's blog. Key works: [Low rattling: A predictive principle for self-organization in active collectives (2021)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.abc6182); [Causal Geometry (2021)](https://doi.org/10.3390/e23010024); [Emergent order from mixed chaos at low temperature (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2509.11583).
- [Jacob G. Foster](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=dpuY5hkAAAAJ&hl=en), Indiana University and Santa Fe Institute. He works on complex systems, computational social science and collective intelligence. His best-known papers model how scientists choose problems. He gave a talk at the symposium. Key works: [Culture and computation: Steps to a Probably Approximately Correct theory of culture (2018)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.poetic.2018.04.007); [From Thin to Thick: Toward a politics of human-compatible AI (2023)](https://doi.org/10.1215/08992363-10742593); [Tradition and Innovation in Scientists' Research Strategies (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1177/0003122415601618); [Choosing experiments to accelerate collective discovery (2015)](https://doi.org/10.1073/pnas.1509757112).
- [Soojung Yang](https://sites.google.com/view/soojungy), Stanford University (Duke University from 2027). She builds AI methods for discovery in biochemistry. Her talk asks whether models trained on molecules, materials, proteins and language converge on one representation of matter. She gave a talk at the symposium. Key works: [Universally Converging Representations of Matter Across Scientific Foundation Models (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2512.03750); [Scalable emulation of protein equilibrium ensembles with generative deep learning (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1126/science.adv9817); [PIGNet: a physics-informed deep learning model toward generalized drug-target interaction predictions (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1039/d1sc06946b).
- [Hananel Hazan](https://allencenter.tufts.edu/hananel-hazan-ph-d/), Allen Discovery Center, Tufts University. He models how cells cooperate toward shared goals with machine learning and evolutionary search. He studies what groups of cells can compute. He works in Levin's lab, co-wrote many papers with him and co-organized the symposium. Key works: [BindsNET: A Machine Learning-Oriented Spiking Neural Networks Library in Python (2018)](https://doi.org/10.3389/fninf.2018.00089); [Exploring the Behavior of Bioelectric Circuits Using Evolution Heuristic Search (2022)](https://doi.org/10.1089/bioe.2022.0033); [Heuristically Adaptive Diffusion-Model Evolutionary Strategy (2026)](https://doi.org/10.1002/advs.202511537).
- [Nora Belrose](https://scholar.google.com/citations?user=p_oBc64AAAAJ&hl=en), EleutherAI. She works on how to interpret neural networks and language models. Her work includes reading out the hidden predictions of a transformer and erasing concepts from representations. She had a conversation with Levin on his channel about AI, sentience and Platonic space. Key works: [Eliciting Latent Predictions from Transformers with the Tuned Lens (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2303.08112); [LEACE: Perfect linear concept erasure in closed form (2023)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.03819); [Sparse Autoencoders Trained on the Same Data Learn Different Features (2025)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2501.16615).
- [Marsha Hewitt](https://www.trinity.utoronto.ca/profiles/hewitt-marsha/), University of Toronto. She works on psychoanalytic theory and the psychology of religion, including dreams, trance and possession. She also works on critical theory of religion. She had a discussion with Levin on his channel about Platonic space. Key works: [Freud on Religion (2014)](https://doi.org/10.4324/9781315729435); [Legacies of the Occult: Psychoanalysis, Religion, and Unconscious Communication (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1558/isbn.9781781799475); [Trance, Dreams, and Possession (2024)](https://doi.org/10.1093/9780197679951.003.0020).
- [Joscha Bach](https://cimcai.substack.com/p/the-beginning-of-the-california-institute), California Institute for Machine Consciousness. He builds computer models of cognition, motivation and emotion. His institute aims at testable theories of machine consciousness. He had a working session and a podcast with Levin and gave a talk on his channel. Key works: [Principles of Synthetic Intelligence PSI: An Architecture of Motivated Cognition (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1093/acprof:oso/9780195370676.001.0001); [A Framework for Emergent Emotions, Based on Motivation and Cognitive Modulators (2012)](https://doi.org/10.4018/jse.2012010104); [Building Artificial Intelligence with Creative Agency and Self-hood (2024)](https://arxiv.org/abs/2407.10978).

He had a working session with Levin, and his book The Integrated Mind is on Levin's reading list. ## Labs he lists as of interest {#labs-he-lists-as-of-interest}

- [Adam E. Cohen](https://cohenweb.rc.fas.harvard.edu/), Harvard University. His lab builds optical tools to measure and control the voltage of cell membranes. It studies electrical signals in nerves and in other tissues, and physical forces in embryos. He gave a talk on Levin's channel, and Levin cites his work on electrical spikes in bacteria. Key works: [All-optical electrophysiology in mammalian neurons using engineered microbial rhodopsins (2014)](https://doi.org/10.1038/nmeth.3000); [Optically controlled oscillators in an engineered bioelectric tissue (2016)](https://doi.org/10.1103/physrevx.6.031001); [Bioelectrical domain walls in homogeneous tissues (2020)](https://doi.org/10.1038/s41567-019-0765-4).
- [Alex Mogilner](https://cims.nyu.edu/~mogilner/), Courant Institute and Department of Biology, New York University. He builds mathematical and computer models of how cells move and divide. He also studies how cells migrate in electric fields. Levin cites one paper by his group. Key works: [Mathematics of cell motility: have we got its number? (2009)](https://doi.org/10.1007/s00285-008-0182-2); [Electrophoresis of cellular membrane components creates the directional cue guiding keratocyte galvanotaxis (2013)](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.cub.2013.02.047); [Modern modeling of single-cell migration: from membrane tension and galvanotaxis to machine learning (2025)](https://doi.org/10.1101/cshperspect.a041745).
- [Allyson E. Sgro](https://www.janelia.org/people/allyson-sgro), HHMI Janelia Research Campus. Her lab combines theory and experiment to study how groups of cells sense their surroundings and coordinate. It links fast signals inside single cells to slower behaviour of the group.
