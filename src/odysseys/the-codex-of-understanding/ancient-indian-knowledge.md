> [!note] How to acquire knowledge
> We have 6 Pramanas
> - Pratyaksha → through self-experience, direct perception
> - Anumaan → through logical conclusions, inference or indirect perception
> - Upmaan → examples or similarities/analogy
> - Arthapatti → through factual extrapolation, postulation, derivation from circumstances
> - Anupalabdi → through negative conclusion, non-perception, absence of proof
> - Sabda → through written & spoken words from aaptpurush/experts

### Dharma

All dharma shastras are rooted in dharma sutras which are part of kalpa vedanga. Dharma is which holds or which sustains: justice, lawful, sustainability.

- [Yājñavalkya Smṛti](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Y%C4%81j%C3%B1avalkya_Sm%E1%B9%9Bti)
- There are types of them, and the one that still works flawlessly should be svadharma.
- Manu, Vishnu, Parasar, Brihaspati, Katyayana, Gutama, Vyasa and Narada Smrti, Tirukkural.
- Dharmasutras → Vasishtha, Gautama, Baudhāyana, Āpastamba, Hārīta, Hiraṇyakeśi, Vaikhānasa, Viṣṇu, Śaṅkha-Likhita, Auśanasa.

### Artha

The [Arthashastra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Arthashastra) of [Kauṭilya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Chanakya), the Kamandakiya Nitisara, Brihaspati Sutra, and Sukra Niti, Artha Sangraha, **Vidur Niti**.

### Kama

Desire, enjoyment, relishness. The [Kamasutra](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Kamasutra) of [Vātsyāyana](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/V%C4%81tsy%C4%81yana) is most well known. **Jayamangala by Yashodhara**, **Koka Shastra by Koka Pandit**. Other texts include [Ratirahasya](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ratirahasya), Smaradipika, Ratimanjari, Ratiratnapradipika, [Ananga Ranga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Ananga_Ranga), among others: Manasollasa, **Kandarpa Sutra by Vatsyayana Mallanaga**, **Kama Puraanam**, **Smara Pradipa by Gunakara**, **Gandharva Tantra**, [Kam Kunj](https://archive.org/details/in.ernet.dli.2015.345944/page/n9/mode/2up), [Paururav](https://www.exoticindiaart.com/book/details/kama-shastra-grantha-nze437/), and the Śṛṅgārarasaprabandhadīpikā, a Kama Shastra Granth.

### Moksha

Major treatises on the pursuit of moksha include the [Upanishads](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Upanishad), [Vivekachudamani](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Vivekachudamani), [Bhagavad Gita](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Bhagavad_Gita), and the sastras on [Yoga](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Yoga), in particular the fourth portion of Patanjali's Sutras (Yog Sutras), Brahma Sutras.

### The Five-fold Structure of Ayurvedic Knowledge

1. Sutras are fundamental and general Instructions.
2. Nidana is finding root cause -> diagnosis
3. Sareer Sthana -> anatomy of human body to minutest details
4. Chikitsa -> deals with identifying, formulating and preparation of medicines
5. Kalpa Sthana -> Analyzing the impact of poision and preparing the antidotes.
