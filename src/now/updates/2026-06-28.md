Highlight of my month has been ICRA, and I'm writing about my experience
[here](/writings/icra-2026/)

Just a day ago, Germany saw its hottest day, at 41.5 degrees. It's really
unbearable, and the moment you step out you start to sweat — playing out in the
field is even more taxing. Hope this ends soon!

I'm happy things are progressing nicely. I'm nearly wrapping up my hiwi work and
starting to work on my thesis, while trying to manage Frisbee and Salsa once a
week. The heat is so bad that I don't even feel like writing 😓.

I've been able to read very little lately, but I'm really enjoying *Platonic*
and *The Idea Factory*. It's unfortunate that schools don't teach the former,
and that science/engineering students aren't taught the latter. I always feel
we're missing the big picture.

### Things I found interesting

- I'm a fan of Michael Levin's ideas about intelligence, which were recently
  featured on [Quanta: Intelligence Without
  Brains](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=J-HDHwEJ2z8), and I'm glad that he
  uploads some of his lectures and thesis presentations on his channel. (I'm a
  big fan of his ideas about life in general, but to me it's fascinating to
  explore and understand them better. It's always better to read critics and
  make yourself aware of promising counterarguments. I found this [thread on
  Reddit](https://www.reddit.com/r/AskBiology/comments/1p6d0ou/is_michael_levins_work_legitimate/),
  but on an initial skim it looks more like people fighting over already-held
  beliefs without being open.)

- After a long time, I got to watch the second lecture of [Human Behavioral
  Biology](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=5izfiuLvRpk) by Sapolsky. Worth
  watching, although I disagree sometimes.

- And how could I forget [Are We Clueless About the Origin Of
  Life?](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=KvGdllx9pJU) — totally unexpected!!
  I've never seen two people fight like kids in such a situation. Fun to watch;
  I had very little clue about what they were talking about, but I wish I had a
  better grip on the subject so I could better judge their arguments and make up
  my mind.

- And then there are disappointments of the kind that leave me heartbroken. I
  watched [Sanskrit scholar exposes pseudoscience
  peddlers](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=ZDCWEbsSdfs), and a few days later I
  got recommended [Vedic Maths in Quantum Computers? | The Truth
  Revealed](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=D-1VN4WAxRA) by HyperQuest. I used
  to watch his videos and was curious how he was going to respond to the
  comments made in the first video about Vedic maths being framed the wrong way.
  It was surprising to see him spread nonsense to justify our culture and Vedic
  maths. To quote them, the basic claim Nityananda Misra makes is that "Vedic
  maths is okayish and could be used for recreational purposes, but isn't
  something that will help you clear international math olympiads," and
  HyperQuest's reasoning is, "If Vedic math doesn't have any use, then why is one
  of the leading tech companies, IBM, spending crores to make their quantum
  computers more efficient and fast by using Vedic maths?" He presents a
  [paper](https://www.nature.com/articles/s41598-025-00494-5) which is good, but
  when I checked it, it's written by two authors, both from a university in Iran
  with no affiliation to IBM; they just tested it on an IBM machine — it could
  have been any quantum computer. The claim they make is much narrower than what
  HyperQuest implies. This is misleading and a misuse of scientific literature. I
  can reason through this and say he's making a wrong claim, but what's
  heartbreaking is seeing positive comments supporting him. He did his B.Tech at
  IIT Patna; I wonder what kind of education system we have.

Yeah, recently I've seen a lot of such things (even in the West) that have no
grounding in reality, and it sucks when people don't want to admit it even after
being called out, or do proper research before making claims in the future. I've written to
some degree about this in [what we could do better](/writings/educational-reforms-we-need/).

See ya, until next time!
