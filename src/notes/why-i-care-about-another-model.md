Things are moving fast. The edge remains in being able to figure out what's going to grow.

I can't let this happen like it did with coding harnesses, where you wait until it's already blown up and you're kinda behind in using it.

Building good judgement is going to be increasingly important, and the best way to improve it is by practicing. I applied for early access, which I got. Now I want to see for myself what this means for my current workflow and what capacity it has to change or benefit the workflow. Basically, I try to use it and have an opinion. Let time pass and see how things play out. Did I get it right or wrong, and for what reasons?

The better your intuition gets, the better your judgement on anything new coming to market.

After a long time, I've personally gone through docs. It feels refreshing. To be honest, it didn't take more than 15 minutes to skim through most of it, at least the important parts. You eventually get better at skimming and figuring out what to read.

On first impressions, I like it for a few reasons:

- It gives confidence scores and routes requests accordingly. I can see combining this model with something cheap like DeepSeek Flash. Many times, you don't need higher reasoning at all.
- I'm looking forward to open source models trained with [RLCD](https://docs.typesafe.ai/introduction/machine-learning-primer). Not much has been released, but it would be fun to reverse engineer how RLCD works and train small local models.
- The confidence threshold is a hard number and can get difficult to tweak. I believe logging and feeding that back should improve the system. Again, this is a bit of a limitation, I believe. You would lean towards a lower threshold in critical systems and get false negatives, or if you make it higher, you risk it all.
